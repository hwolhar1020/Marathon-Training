Fuel

Nutrition

The basics

  • Targets are a daily average for the week, set from your size, age and training load.
  • Protein 130–135 g/day, split into 4 feedings of 30–40 g.
  • Fat about 75–80 g/day. Carbs rise with mileage.
  • Fiber 30–38 g/day. Water about 3 L/day plus run fluids.
  • Weigh in 2× a week. Losing more than 1 lb/week for 2+ weeks, or feeling flat? Add 200 kcal, mostly carbs.
  • Want to lean out? In weeks 2–13 only, trim about 250 kcal on rest and lifting days, never protein. No deficit from week 14 on.

Long runs: start fueling 30–40 min in, then every 25–35 min. One gel ≈ 20–25 g carbs. Add 300–600 mg sodium per hour on runs over 90 min.

Sample peak-week day · ~2,800 kcal

  • Breakfast: 1 cup oats in milk, banana, 1 tbsp peanut butter, 2 eggs (~750 kcal, 35 g protein).
  • Lunch: Burrito bowl, 6 oz chicken, 1½ cups rice, beans, salsa, cheese (~800 kcal, 55 g).
  • Afternoon: Greek yogurt, granola, berries (~350 kcal, 20 g).
  • Pre-run: Toast with honey (~200 kcal).
  • Dinner: 5 oz salmon, potatoes, roasted vegetables, olive oil (~700 kcal, 35 g).

Real life happens

Adjusting

  • Missed a run? Skip it; don't stack it. Priority: Saturday, Thursday, Tuesday, Wednesday.
  • Only 3 days? Drop Wednesday.
  • Long run won't fit Saturday? Sunday works. Rest Monday and move Strength A to Tuesday evening.
  • Pain: Stop if it's sharp, changes your stride, or worsens as you run. Take 2–3 days off. Lasts over a week? See a physical therapist or sports doctor.
  • Sick: Above-the-neck symptoms, easy run is fine. Fever or chest symptoms, rest.
  • Shoes: Replace around 300–500 mi.
  • Fitness check: Monthly, note your easy pace at HR ~150. It should drop toward ~9:45 by March.
